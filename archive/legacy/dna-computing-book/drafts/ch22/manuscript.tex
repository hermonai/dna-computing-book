\chapter{Self-assembly, tiles and geometry}

A reaction network computes in a well-mixed state: species identities and counts
matter, but two molecules do not normally carry a permanent north, south, east and
west. Self-assembly adds an address. A component can expose different recognition
interfaces on different sides, and the identity of the next component can depend on
the interfaces already present at a growth front. The new question is therefore
spatial: \emph{can local recognition rules force a desired global object?}

This chapter answers that question first in a deliberately small formal model and
then asks what survives when the tiles are molecules. The distinction matters.
An abstract glue is a symbol with an integer strength. A sticky end is a sequence
whose association depends on concentration, temperature, salt, secondary structure,
competing strands and time. A proof about the first object becomes a molecular
claim only after an implementation establishes the required correspondence.

\section{A precise seeded tile model}

A \Term{tile type} is an oriented unit square with four sides
$D=\{N,E,S,W\}$. Each side carries a pair $(\ell,s)$ consisting of a glue label
$\ell$ and nonnegative integer strength $s$. Rotation is not allowed in this
chapter. An \Term{assembly} is a finite partial function
$\alpha:\mathbb Z^2\rightharpoonup T$ from lattice positions to tile types.
The seed $\sigma$ is an initial finite assembly.
Fix a glue dictionary assigning one strength to each label. The null label has
strength zero. Our code rejects a bond between equal labels with unequal
strengths: taking one side's strength would make support evaluation asymmetric.

For adjacent occupied positions, a bond contributes strength $s$ exactly when
the two facing labels are equal, non-null, and the declared strengths agree.
At temperature $\tau=2$, a tile may attach at an empty position $p$ when the
sum of matching strengths to already occupied north, east, south and west
neighbors is at least two. Figure~\ref{fig:tile} shows the contract.

\Plate{01-tile-contract}{An oriented abstract tile and the local attachment
predicate. The model adds integer strengths; it does not assign a molecular
free energy to those integers.}{fig:tile}

A one-tile extension is written $\alpha\to\beta$. An assembly is
\Term{producible} if a finite sequence of legal extensions starts at $\sigma$.
For a conventional seeded aTAM system, the seed must be $\tau$-stable:
every cut separating its binding graph into two nonempty parts has total crossing
strength at least $\tau$. Connectivity alone is insufficient. A strength-one
chain is connected but has a strength-one cut at $\tau=2$.
Adding a tile with support at least $\tau$ preserves stability: a cut isolating
that tile crosses its full support; any other cut already separates the old
stable assembly and gains only nonnegative additional bonds.

It is \Term{terminal} if no tile can legally attach anywhere on its frontier.
A seeded system is \Term{directed} when every maximal legal assembly sequence
has the same terminal assembly. Unique choice at each visited frontier site is
sufficient for our finite construction, but directedness is a property of the
whole transition system, not merely of one preferred schedule.

\section{Build XOR into geometry}

Let each computational tile receive a bit $s$ from its south side and a bit $w$
from its west side. Define four tile types
\[
X_{sw}:\quad S=v_s,\quad W=h_w,\quad N=v_{s\oplus w},
\quad E=h_{s\oplus w},
\]
with all four computational glues of strength one. Because $\tau=2$, neither
input alone is enough: a tile must agree with both already present inputs.

Use an L-shaped seed. Along the bottom boundary expose vertical bits
$1,0,0,\ldots$; along the left boundary expose horizontal zeros. For interior
coordinates $(x,y)$ with $x,y\ge1$, the tile bit therefore satisfies
\begin{equation}
 z_{x,y}=z_{x,y-1}\oplus z_{x-1,y}.
 \label{dnad22:xor}
\end{equation}
This is Pascal's recurrence modulo two in a rotated coordinate frame.
The two arms and corner are joined by seed-only strength-two bonds. The
computational sides still expose strength-one bit glues; the strong seed bonds
must not match the computational alphabet. Every cut of this connected seed
crosses a strength-two bond. The seed is supplied as a preassembled input
object, not grown by the four computational types.

\begin{proposition}[Unique interior tile]
Assume the south and west neighbors of an empty interior position are present,
expose $v_s$ and $h_w$, and its north/east positions are empty.
Exactly one reference tile can attach
at temperature two, namely $X_{sw}$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Tile $X_{sw}$ matches both occupied inputs, so its total support is $1+1=2$.
Any other $X_{s'w'}$ differs in at least one input bit. If exactly one differs,
the support is one; if both differ, it is zero. Both are below $\tau=2$.
Thus the legal tile is unique.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Order-independent finite growth]
For the stable size-$n$ seed and the four specified nonrotating XOR types at
$\tau=2$, every maximal assembly sequence has the same terminal assembly:
the seed and precisely the $n^2$ interior cells satisfying
Equation~\ref{dnad22:xor}.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let $I$ be the occupied interior positions. Inductively $I$ is a lower ideal:
if $(x,y)\in I$, all interior positions southwest of it are in $I$.
At a missing interior position, an occupied north or east neighbor would
contradict this property. Only south and west can contribute, and both
must be occupied to supply two units. The preceding proposition fixes
one tile and preserves the lower-ideal property. No position outside the
square can gain two computational contacts: beyond either arm only one
inward neighbor is possible, and the boundary's other exposed glues are null
or seed-only. If the square is incomplete, a minimal missing position has
both predecessors present and admits an addition. A maximal sequence
therefore fills the square; it cannot stop at a smaller ideal. The recurrence
uniquely fixes every bit by induction on $x+y$.
\end{proof}

This proof quantifies over all schedules. It explains why the north/east
assumption in the local proposition is legitimate here but not for an arbitrary
assembly. The code compares the square with an independent cellular-automaton oracle.

Exhaustive exploration merges identical partial assemblies rather than replaying
every permutation of additions. The occupied lower ideals of an $n$-by-$n$
square correspond to monotone lattice boundary paths, giving
$\binom{2n}{n}$ states: $20$ for $n=3$ and $70$ for $n=4$. The code finds one
terminal state for each tested size $0\le n\le4$ without clipping its frontier
to the square. A separate small-graph cut oracle checks seed and product
stability. These finite checks corroborate the proof; an exploration-budget
exception is not a termination certificate.

The first rows for $n=8$ are generated in \texttt{results.json}; they are not
hand-copied artwork. The test suite also attempts an attachment before its west
input exists and confirms that one strength-one bond cannot cross $\tau=2$.

\section{Local legality is not global determinism}

Now add one mutant tile with the same south and west input glues as $X_{10}$ but
a zero output bit and north/east interfaces $v_0,h_0$, rather than the correct
one bit and $v_1,h_1$. At a site receiving south $1$ and west
$0$, both tiles have support two. The simulator returns two legal candidates.

\Plate{03-ambiguity}{A single extra tile type can preserve local legality while
destroying unique continuation. Directedness therefore requires reasoning about
the set of all legal growth paths, not just replaying one successful trace.}{fig:ambiguity}

This counterexample is small but important. A renderer that always chooses the
first legal tile can still draw a perfect Sierpiński pattern and conceal the
model error. Verification must enumerate the legal set or compare the transition
relation with an independent specification. The same principle appeared in
Chapter 20: a deterministic numerical integrator does not prove that the modeled
chemistry has only one relevant pathway.

Mismatch is a separate notion. In our aTAM variant a tile can attach with total
support two while exposing a nonmatching glue to another occupied neighbor if
the two matching sides already supply enough support. One can forbid such
mismatches by definition, penalize them in a kinetic model, or allow them.
Those are different models and can have different terminal assemblies.

\section{Resources: tile types, area, frontier and material}

For an $n\times n$ computation the reference construction uses four computational
tile types independent of $n$, but places $n^2$ computational tile instances.
The distinction is analogous to program size versus execution work:
\[
\text{tile complexity}=4,\qquad
\text{placement work}=n^2,\qquad
\text{final area}=n^2\ \text{lattice cells}.
\]
The seed itself contains $2n+1$ boundary instances in this finite presentation.
A four-type claim counts only computational types, not the whole device.
The reference names each boundary tile separately; its full type inventory
is $4+(2n+1)$. Reusing identical boundary species could reduce that description,
but does not remove the $O(n)$ input-seed preparation.
A physical implementation must additionally supply enough copies of each tile
species to avoid depletion over the intended growth ensemble.

If each of $N=n^2$ placements were independently correct with probability
$1-p$, then the probability of a completely correct square would be
\begin{equation}
P_{\rm all}=(1-p)^N.
\label{dnad22:yield}
\end{equation}
This is an explanatory finite-size model, not a law of tile growth: real errors
can be correlated, growth-front dependent and reversible. It nevertheless shows
why a small per-placement error does not imply high object yield. At $p=0.01$,
a $10\times10$ object has all-correct probability about $0.366$, while a
$100\times100$ object is essentially never perfect under the independent model.
The numerical values are generated by the reference program.
\begin{center}\input{\Generated/yield_examples.tex}\end{center}
The $100$-by-$100$ result is extremely small, not mathematically zero. Code uses
$\exp[N\log(1-p)]$ with \texttt{log1p} for small $p$, and separately handles
zero placements and probability endpoints.

A second resource is time. A maximally parallel abstract schedule can grow many
sites on one diagonal concurrently, giving $O(n)$ dependency depth even though
there are $O(n^2)$ placements. A serial simulator takes $O(n^2)$ attachment
steps. Neither count is a measured assembly time. Molecular time depends on
collision rates, concentrations, detachment and transport.

\section{From glue labels to DNA tile interfaces}

Algorithmic DNA tile experiments instantiate spatial interfaces with DNA motifs.
A schematic double-crossover tile contains antiparallel duplex segments joined
by crossover connections; single-stranded sticky ends can expose sequence-specific
recognition at selected edges. Figure~\ref{fig:dx} isolates the sticky-end
interface: exposed complementary domains associate while preserving strand
polarity and leaving the backbones unligated. It is not a complete
double-crossover strand-routing design.

\Plate{04-molecular-map}{A molecular edge-recognition mechanism. Continuous
lines are independent covalent backbones; dashed red marks are newly paired
overhang bases. Arrowheads point to $3'$ ends. Two nicks remain: hybridization
is not ligation. A contact is not automatically an integer glue strength or
a measured $\Delta G$.}{fig:dx}

The 2004 Sierpiński-triangle experiment translated an XOR cellular-automaton
rule into double-crossover DNA tiles and observed recognizable finite fractal
patterns by atomic-force microscopy~\cite{sierpinski}. That result is historically decisive
because it closes more of the abstraction ladder than a simulation does:
logical rule $\rightarrow$ tile identities $\rightarrow$ molecular motifs
$\rightarrow$ assembled structures $\rightarrow$ instrument image. It also
reported substantial assembly errors, which is exactly why abstract
directedness must not be confused with physical yield.

The mapping has at least four layers:
\begin{enumerate}
\item an abstract side says which labels are compatible;
\item sequence design chooses candidate recognition domains;
\item thermodynamics and kinetics determine association and dissociation under
      an experimental condition;
\item imaging/classification decides whether the resulting object counts as a
      correct assembly.
\end{enumerate}
Failure can enter at every arrow. Two sticky ends intended to be orthogonal can
cross-hybridize; a correct contact can detach; a weakly supported incorrect tile
can become kinetically trapped by later growth; an aggregate can nucleate away
from the seed; an imaging pipeline can misclassify a defect.

\section{Origami changes the geometry contract}

Scaffolded DNA origami solves a different but complementary problem. A long
single-stranded scaffold is routed through a desired shape and many shorter
staple strands bind separated scaffold segments, constraining the scaffold into
the designed geometry. The computation is therefore not naturally described as
one square tile choosing the next square tile. The design object is a routed
polymer plus a set of staple constraints.

\Plate{06-origami-route}{A routed scaffold and three distinct staples.
Each staple pairs antiparallel to separated domains and constrains their
relative placement. Continuity, staple identity and base pairing are different
relations. Helix twist, junction phasing and sequence design are omitted;
this mechanism cartoon is not a folding protocol or atomically resolved shape.}{fig:origami}

Rothemund's 2006 work~\cite{origami} demonstrated roughly hundred-nanometre two-dimensional
shapes and addressable patterns using a roughly 7-kilobase scaffold and more
than two hundred staples. For this chapter the important conceptual point is
not the exact historical dimensions; it is that geometry can be encoded by
global scaffold routing plus local hybridization constraints. Tile assembly
and origami can also be composed: an origami object can act as a larger
addressable component, while tiles can extend or decorate structures.

Thus ``DNA self-assembly'' does not name one computational model. A rigorous
description states what is programmable: tile adjacency, scaffold route,
staple occupancy, strand-displacement pathway, or some combination.

\section{Why physical growth is not monotone}

The aTAM used above is irreversible: once a legal tile attaches, it never leaves.
Molecular association is generally reversible. A minimal kinetic picture assigns
attachment and detachment propensities that depend on free tile concentration
and binding context. Stronger cooperative contacts can reduce detachment, but
the growth front is then a stochastic process over assemblies rather than a
monotone partial order.

Consider a wrong tile after a weak initial contact. Let it detach with rate
$k_{\rm off}$, or acquire a stabilizing neighboring contact with rate
$k_{\rm lock}$. Both are in $\mathrm{s}^{-1}$ and are stipulated constant
exponential clocks during this local episode. Residence survival is
$S(t)=e^{-(k_{\rm off}+k_{\rm lock})t}$. The locking probability is
\begin{equation}
 P_{\rm lock}=\int_0^\infty k_{\rm lock}S(t)\,dt
 =\frac{k_{\rm lock}}{k_{\rm off}+k_{\rm lock}}.
 \label{dnad22:trap}
\end{equation}
Mean time to either event is $1/(k_{\rm off}+k_{\rm lock})$. With rates
$9$ and $1$, one in ten such contacts locks. If stronger wrong binding
reduces $k_{\rm off}$ to $1$ while $k_{\rm lock}$ is unchanged, the probability
rises to one half. This is conditional on a wrong contact, not a defect
fraction of all products. Arrival frequency and later repair are additional factors.

In an ideal dilute solution, the association hazard of one free tile species
can be modeled as $k_{\rm on}c$, with $k_{\rm on}$ in
$\mathrm{M}^{-1}\mathrm{s}^{-1}$ and free concentration $c$ in molar units.
Assigning rates to glues requires more than an integer-strength lookup:
depletion, cooperative contacts and geometric accessibility can change the
state space as well as its hazards.

\Plate{05-physical-ladder}{Competing exits from a wrong initial contact.
Detachment rejects it; a second contact can trap it. Arrows denote transition
hazards, not covalent bonds. This local rate model is conditional on contact,
not an observed assembly-yield curve.}{fig:physical}

Three failure modes deserve separate names. \Term{Spurious nucleation} starts
growth without the intended seed. A \Term{kinetic trap} is a state that is
reachable quickly but escapes slowly relative to the observation window.
A \Term{mismatch} is an unintended local interface relation. Their remedies can
conflict: lowering concentration can suppress some unwanted attachments while
also slowing desired growth; stronger bonds can stabilize correct contacts while
also making an incorrect contact harder to remove.

Proofreading tile constructions~\cite{proofreading} exploit cooperativity by replacing one logical
tile with a structured block of physical tiles so that an isolated error has
difficulty acquiring enough subsequent support. This is an architectural
error-control idea, not a guarantee that any chosen sequences achieve a target
error rate. Chapter 23 will make that distinction quantitative.

\section{A practical verification protocol}

A useful self-assembly claim should be testable at all layers. For the formal
system, enumerate small assemblies and check directedness, terminality and the
claimed output function. For the sequence layer, test intended and unintended
interactions under a declared thermodynamic model. For kinetics, vary
concentration and temperature and compare time-resolved predictions with
controls. For physical products, define the observation rule before looking at
the data and report defect distributions, not only representative images.

The package's executable tests cover the first layer only. They prove neither
sequence orthogonality nor experimental yield. That is a feature of the
evidence contract: the code says exactly what it knows.

\section{Exercises with worked answers}

\begin{enumerate}
\item At $\tau=2$, can one strength-one input attach an XOR tile?
\textbf{Answer:} No. Its support is one.
\item Prove that $X_{sw}$ is unique when south and west inputs are present.
\textbf{Answer:} With north/east empty, it has support two; every other tile
mismatches an input and has support at most one. Additional occupied neighbors
require a separate argument.
\item Why does a successful raster-order simulation not prove directedness?
\textbf{Answer:} The simulator may hide alternative legal attachments by its
tie-breaking rule. Directedness concerns every maximal legal sequence.
\item With $p=0.01$ independent placement error and $N=100$, compute all-correct
yield. \textbf{Answer:} $0.99^{100}\approx0.366$.
\item What happens as $N\to\infty$ for fixed $0<p<1$?
\textbf{Answer:} $(1-p)^N\to0$.
\item Distinguish tile complexity from assembly size.
\textbf{Answer:} Tile complexity counts distinct programmable types; assembly
size counts placed instances. Four types can generate arbitrarily large finite
prefixes when the seed/boundary permits them.
\item Why is abstract strength 2 not ``twice the binding energy'' of strength 1?
\textbf{Answer:} Strength is a discrete model parameter. Molecular free energy
depends on sequence and condition and combines nonlinearly with entropy and
concentration.
\item Give a mismatch that is still attachable.
\textbf{Answer:} A tile may match south and west with total support two while
mismatching an already occupied north neighbor, if the model permits mismatches.
\item What does spurious nucleation violate?
\textbf{Answer:} The intended seeded-growth contract: an assembly begins away
from the designated seed.
\item Why can stronger binding worsen a kinetic trap?
\textbf{Answer:} It can reduce the escape/detachment rate of an incorrect state.
\item Compare origami and square-tile programming.
\textbf{Answer:} Origami primarily programs a global scaffold route constrained
by staples; square-tile models program local adjacency rules. They can be
combined but are not identical abstractions.
\item Design an experiment testing the abstract-to-physical mapping.
\textbf{Rubric:} Declare sequences and conditions; include seeded/no-seed and
mismatch controls; measure time and defect distributions; vary concentration or
temperature; define image classification in advance; compare with a reversible
kinetic model; report uncertainty and failed assemblies.
\item Why is the new seed temperature-two stable?
\textbf{Answer:} Its seed-only graph is connected and every edge has strength
two. Each nontrivial cut crosses an edge. The one-vertex size-zero seed has no
nontrivial cut.
\item How many named full-device types does the size-eight reference use?
\textbf{Answer:} Four computational types plus $2(8)+1$ seed types, or $21$.
This is a description count, not the number of material copies.
\item Derive the probability that detachment wins the two-clock race.
\textbf{Answer:} Integrate $k_{\rm off}e^{-(k_{\rm off}+k_{\rm lock})t}$;
the result is $k_{\rm off}/(k_{\rm off}+k_{\rm lock})$ for positive total
rate. If both rates vanish, there is no exit and the ratio is undefined.
\item Why does two-domain staple binding not certify an origami shape?
\textbf{Answer:} Scaffold routing, required occupancies, helix twist and junction
geometry must all agree. Local pairing does not establish global folding or yield.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Vocabulary and next interface}

\Term{Directed assembly}: all maximal legal growth paths converge to the same
terminal assembly. \Term{Frontier}: empty lattice positions adjacent to the
current assembly. \Term{Cooperative attachment}: multiple contacts jointly cross
the attachment threshold. \Term{Spurious nucleation}: unintended seed-independent
growth. \Term{Kinetic trap}: a long-lived off-pathway state on the experimental
timescale.

Chapter 23 asks what to do when the local rule is correct but physical execution
is not. Redundancy, proofreading, detection and repair will be treated as
resource-bearing mechanisms rather than as a generic promise of robustness.
