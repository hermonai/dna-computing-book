# Chapter 15: Splicing and insertion-deletion systems

Author-approved storyboard for the original standalone candidate. Independent review remains open.

## 01-splice

Question: How do contextual cuts determine both recombinant products?
Objects and arrow semantics: Two oriented parent strings, marked cut sites, and exchanged suffixes; solid strand backbones, dashed pairing only when duplex is explicit.
Change/invariant and caption claim: The sum of both product lengths is conserved; one displayed product is not the entire material balance.
Scientific risk: Do not equate this symbolic operation with RNA splicing or a specific enzyme protocol.

## 02-context

Question: Why does a local insertion need context and a payload source?
Objects and arrow semantics: A left and right address flank an insertion boundary; the payload appears as a distinct donor, then a changed string.
Change/invariant and caption claim: Only the selected boundary changes; contexts survive the insertion.
Scientific risk: Formal string lengths are not a complete nucleotide inventory.

## 03-branch

Question: Does one rewrite path describe every reachable word?
Objects and arrow semantics: A branching derivation tree with repeated words merged by equality and a marked depth boundary.
Change/invariant and caption claim: Paths are alternative derivations, not simultaneous physical copies.
Scientific risk: A truncated search is not a proof of non-reachability.

## 04-duplex

Question: What molecular constraints does concatenation omit?
Objects and arrow semantics: Two duplex fragments with complementary cohesive ends, annealed intermediate and sealed backbones.
Change/invariant and caption claim: Pairing aligns ends; ligation joins compatible backbone ends.
Scientific risk: Generic geometry only, not sequence- or enzyme-specific reaction design.

## 05-budget

Question: Which search costs grow before chemistry begins?
Objects and arrow semantics: An original calculated curve of distinct words by derivation depth and explicit bounded-search output.
Change/invariant and caption claim: Depth and word-count caps stop the explorer without asserting language closure.
Scientific risk: Counts are symbolic search results, not molecular yield.
