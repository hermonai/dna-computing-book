# Deep Chapter 1 source and originality review

Access/review date: 7 September 2026. Author-agent review, not independent endorsement. Only the portions named below were used. The bibliography is selective, not a completed field survey. No source figures, code, or long quotations were copied. Graphs, exact programs, arithmetic, sampling derivation, and memory calculations are original teaching constructions with declared assumptions.

## adleman

[Leonard M. Adleman. Molecular computation of solutions to combinatorial problems. Science 266 (1994), 1021--1024.](https://computingbiology.github.io/docs/adleman1994.pdf)

Access and use: Full relevant article and experimental notes in mixed-article scan; seven-vertex instance, edge/vertex orientation, endpoint PCR, 140-bp selection, affinity stages, graduated PCR and limitations. Original six-vertex example is not a reproduction.

## dna

[Bruce Alberts et al. The Structure and Function of DNA. Molecular Biology of the Cell, 4th edition (2002).](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK26821/)

Access and use: Structure subsection: backbone direction, complementary pairing and antiparallel orientation only; no dated gene counts.

## ligase

[New England Biolabs. T4 DNA Ligase, product description.](https://www.neb.com/en-us/products/m0202-t4-dna-ligase)

Access and use: Mechanism and substrate description: phosphodiester connection at suitable nick ends; no laboratory protocol or vendor performance claim.

## pcr

[National Human Genome Research Institute. Polymerase Chain Reaction Fact Sheet.](https://www.genome.gov/about-genomics/fact-sheets/Polymerase-Chain-Reaction-Fact-Sheet)

Access and use: Fact sheet read; primer-directed amplification mechanism, not perfect selection or guaranteed yield.

## gel

## hamilton

[Frank Pfenning. Lecture 36: The Hamiltonian Path Problem. Carnegie Mellon University, 15-453.](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~fp/courses/flac/lectures/lecture36.html)

Access and use: Course page confirms directed and undirected Hamiltonian-path NP-completeness. Verification and fixed-endpoint counting are independently explained here.
