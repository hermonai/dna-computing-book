# Molecular foundations and prerequisites

Teach only enough chemistry to make each operation understandable, but never replace mechanism with metaphor. D-S12 and D-S13 anchor terminology and structure.

1. Atoms and covalent bonds establish molecular composition; hydrogen bonding is not the covalent backbone.
2. Distinguish base, nucleoside, nucleotide and the residue incorporated in a polymer.
3. Sugar carbon numbering explains 5′/3′ polarity and phosphodiester linkage. Free ends need not universally have a phosphate; circular molecules have no free ends.
4. Antiparallel orientation makes reverse complement necessary when both strands are written 5′→3′.
5. Hybridization competes with alternative structures and depends on sequence, salt, temperature and concentration. GC percentage is not a complete melting predictor.
6. Polymerase, ligase and restriction enzymes enact different substrate-dependent transformations.
7. PCR, electrophoresis, affinity purification, synthesis and sequencing are experimental operations with losses and biases.
8. Transcription, mutation, recombination and regulation connect sequence information to living systems; a cell is not just a string processor.
