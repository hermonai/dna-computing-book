## Figure review before prose

### DNAU-01-F1 — Counting by moving objects

Reader question: What changes when we add two?

Concept: input, step, output. Prerequisites: counting to five.

Objects: three numbered counters; two waiting counters; a receiving tray.

Sequence: Start with three in the tray → Move waiting counter 4 → Move waiting counter 5.

Caption message: The output is five; moving is the operation, counting is the reading.

Scientific risk: Do not create or consume counters between panels.

Evidence: Original arithmetic example.

Internal decision: Approved for drawing: all arrows must say operation, comparison, reading, or teaching order; stable objects retain identity.

### DNAU-01-F2 — One rule, one intended result

Reader question: Why is add more not enough?

Concept: rule; ambiguous instruction. Prerequisites: counting; input and output.

Objects: one instruction card; two interpretations; corrected instruction.

Sequence: Read add more → Compare adding one and adding two → Replace it with add exactly two.

Caption message: An unclear instruction permits different results even when the start is identical.

Scientific risk: Do not imply all computation must have a unique output; this is a deterministic example.

Evidence: Original instruction-design example.

Internal decision: Approved for drawing: all arrows must say operation, comparison, reading, or teaching order; stable objects retain identity.

Caption message: Representations agree only because their reading conventions are stated.
