# DNA Computing: first-principles redesign

Date: 2026-09-05. Status: provisional architecture approved for the next chapter-level research pass, not external peer review.

## New thesis

DNA computing is the deliberate encoding, transformation and readout of information in molecular systems. Its achievements become understandable only when abstract algorithms are connected to chemistry, resource costs and experimental evidence.

## What the previous edition attempted

The old 48-chapter plan combined an introduction to molecular computing with a substantial DNA-inspired neural-model curriculum. Its three drafted chapters foregrounded epistemology and reproducibility before teaching molecular structure. That made the project careful but delayed the beginner's first physical explanation.

## Decisions

Start with one concrete information-processing problem, then teach the molecules needed to understand it. Move reproducibility into worked experiments and a methods appendix. Keep DNA language models as a boundary chapter, not the climax. Give self-assembly, reaction networks, laboratory constraints and storage their own routes through the book.

Keep concepts only after renewed definition and evidence review. No historical sentence is adopted as active chapter prose. Mathematical involutions, explicit cost accounting, causality interventions and provenance remain useful tools, not brand-specific discoveries.

## Audience and prerequisites

A programmer with no molecular biology background is the primary reader. Graduate students get formal models and proofs; experimentalists get precise accounts of what the simulations omit.

Use three explanatory layers: a concrete event, its information transformation, and a formal representation. Introduce each mathematical object with an example before symbolic generalization. The [candidate TOC](BOOK_PLAN.md) is a dependency plan, not a promise that every proposed chapter survives.

## Research scope and limitations

This reset performed a **scoping review**, not an exhaustive 2026 systematic review. Primary-source metadata and abstracts support the map; methods, supplements, quantitative claims and theorem proofs remain gated by chapter-specific review. The source register explicitly records access depth. No new model training, wet-lab experiment, benchmark victory or AGI evidence is claimed.
