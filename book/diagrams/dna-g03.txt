TITLE: From molecule to a defensible computation
SUBTITLE: A result needs an encoding and a measurement contract
EVIDENCE: Editorial model; arrows specify relationships, not measured superiority.
LEGEND: DATA → transformation or dependency; CONTROL ⇢ condition or review; HYPOTHESIS ⇢ unverified proposal.

NODE n1 | Specified problem | Define legal inputs and what counts as an answer.
NODE n2 | Representation and algorithm | Choose encoding, transitions and an output rule.
NODE n3 | Resource prediction | Account for material, timing and expected error.
NODE n4 | Molecular construction | Specify strands, operations and physical assumptions.
NODE n5 | Controlled experiment | Record controls, conditions and raw observations.
NODE n6 | Claim with limits | State only what the measured experiment supports.

EDGE n1 → n2 | DATA | encode
EDGE n2 → n3 | DATA | analyze
EDGE n1 → n4 | CONTROL | implement
EDGE n2 → n5 | CONTROL | test mapping
EDGE n3 → n6 | CONTROL | confront measurement
EDGE n4 → n5 | DATA | execute
EDGE n5 → n6 | DATA | interpret

READING: Unexpected products feed back into the construction and abstraction; preserve the failed record.
FAILURE: An attractive simulation without physical controls is not an experimental demonstration.
