Figure: reading map of the book.

Eight horizontal bands correspond to the eight parts of the book. Under each
band, chapter cards appear in their suggested reading order from left to right,
joined by small arrows. Each card shows the chapter number, a short title,
badges for reading emphasis (C = core path for every reader, T = theory
emphasis, L = laboratory and engineering emphasis), and, where relevant, the
key prerequisites from earlier parts ("needs 6-7").

Part I (Computing with molecules): 1 Computing with DNA (C), 2 Adleman's
experiment (C), 3 Search and complexity (T), 4 Parallelism and resources (C).
Part II (The molecular toolkit): 5 DNA chemistry and geometry (C), 6
Hybridisation thermodynamics (C, L), 7 Kinetics and stochastics (C), 8 Enzymes
as operators (C, L; needs 5), 9 Amplification by PCR (C, L), 10 Separation and
readout (L).
Part III (Encoding and molecular algorithms): 11 Sequence design and encoding
(C, L; needs 5-6), 12 Generate, filter and verify (C; needs 2, 4), 13 SAT
constructions (T; needs 3), 14 Sticker memory (T).
Part IV (Formal models): 15 Splicing and insertion systems (T; needs 8), 16
Languages and automata (T), 17 Watson-Crick automata (T), 18 Universality and
complexity (T; needs 3).
Part V (Programming molecular dynamics): 19 Strand displacement (C, L; needs
6-7), 20 Reaction networks as programs (C, T; needs 7), 21 Circuits and neural
networks (C, L), 22 Self-assembly and origami (C, T, L; needs 6).
Part VI (Reliability, scale and storage): 23 Noise and fault models (L; needs
12, 22), 24 Scaling and resource limits (L; needs 4), 25 DNA data storage (C,
L; needs 9-11), 26 Workflows and reproducibility (L; needs 10).
Part VII (Modelling and simulation): 27 Symbolic simulation (T; needs 20), 28
Kinetic and stochastic simulation (L; needs 7, 20), 29 Differentiable models
(T).
Part VIII (From molecules to genomes): 30 Genomic regulation (C; needs 7, 21),
31 Molecular programming today (C, L; needs 19-21), 32 Molecular to genomic
computation (T), followed by the Epilogue on frontiers and open problems.
