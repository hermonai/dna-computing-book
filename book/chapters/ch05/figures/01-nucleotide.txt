Figure: skeletal structure of one DNA nucleotide, 2'-deoxyadenosine
5'-monophosphate (dAMP), in three tinted regions.

Phosphate (amber, left): a phosphorus atom with one double-bonded oxygen and
two negatively charged oxygens, bonded through a bridging oxygen (O5') to
carbon 5' of the sugar. This is the 5' end of the nucleotide.
Sugar (blue, centre): 2'-deoxyribose drawn as a five-membered ring of four
carbons (1', 2', 3', 4') and one ring oxygen; carbon 5' lies outside the ring
attached to 4'. Carbon 3' carries a hydroxyl group (3'-OH), which in a strand
joins the next nucleotide's phosphate. Carbon 2' carries two hydrogens; RNA has
a hydroxyl group at this position.
Base (green, right): adenine, a purine made of a six-membered ring fused to a
five-membered ring, with ring nitrogens numbered 1, 3, 7 and 9 and an amino
group (NH2) on carbon 6. Nitrogen 9 is bonded to sugar carbon 1' by the
N-glycosidic bond.
The drawing shows connectivity and conventional double bonds; it does not show
three-dimensional shape, sugar pucker or every hydrogen.
