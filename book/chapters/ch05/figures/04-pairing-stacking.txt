Figure: Watson-Crick base pairs as skeletal chemical structures, with an
edge-on stacking inset.

Left pair, A.T (two hydrogen bonds): adenine (green tint, a purine of fused six-
and five-membered rings) faces thymine (red tint, a single six-membered
pyrimidine ring with a methyl group). Dashed rose lines are hydrogen bonds: the
amino hydrogen on adenine's N6 to thymine's O4, and adenine's N1 to the
hydrogen on thymine's N3.
Middle pair, G.C (three hydrogen bonds): guanine (amber tint) faces cytosine
(blue tint). Hydrogen bonds join guanine's O6 to an amino hydrogen of
cytosine's N4, the hydrogen on guanine's N1 to cytosine's N3, and an amino
hydrogen of guanine's N2 to cytosine's O2.
In both pairs the bonds to the sugars ("sugar" labels) leave from the lower,
minor-groove edge; the upper edge faces the major groove.
Right inset: four base pairs seen edge-on between two backbones, stacked about
0.34 nm apart. Stacking between neighbouring pairs contributes substantially to
duplex stability.
Hydrogen-bond lengths are drawn about twice a covalent bond; geometry is
schematic and planar.
