Figure: a splicing rule exchanges the right-hand parts of two cut parents.
Panel A: parent x is made of segments a, u1, u2 and b; parent y of c, v1, v2 and d.
The contexts u1 u2 and v1 v2 fix the cuts (red dashed lines), between u1 and u2 and
between v1 and v2. Exchanging the parts to the right of the cuts gives the first
recombinant a u1 v2 d, which formal-language conventions record, and the
complementary recombinant c v1 u2 b, kept here for material balance. Segment
colours track which parent each part came from.
Panel B: the symbolic example LA|BR and MC|DN gives LADN and MCBR. Nothing is
copied: every symbol of both parents ends up in exactly one product. The letters
are abstract symbols, not nucleotides.
