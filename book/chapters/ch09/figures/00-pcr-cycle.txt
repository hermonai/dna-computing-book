Figure: one PCR cycle and the growth of copy number over many cycles.

Top: a temperature trace runs across three shaded phases. Phase 1, denature
(about 95 C): a duplex (blue top strand 5' to 3', violet bottom strand 3' to
5') separates into two single-stranded templates as heat breaks base pairs.
Phase 2, anneal (about 55 C): short primers pair near the 3' end of each
template: a reverse primer (rose) on the blue strand, pointing left, and a
forward primer (green) on the violet strand, pointing right. Phase 3, extend
(about 72 C): gold polymerases extend each primer 5' to 3' (dashed growth),
giving two duplexes. A looping arrow indicates repetition: every product can
serve as a template in the next cycle.
Bottom left: a log-scale plot of copies against cycle number from 10^3
starting copies. A straight blue line shows ideal doubling; an amber dashed
curve shows an assigned efficiency of 0.85 per cycle followed by a plateau
near 10^10 copies as primers run out. Both are illustrative model curves, not
data.
Bottom right: PCR copies an interval set by two primers, amplifies whatever
matches them, including contaminants and errors, and creates no new
information; efficiency and plateau must be measured.
