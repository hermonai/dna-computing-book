Figure: a conceptual restriction-ligation transition cycle, in three stages.
Stage 1 (anneal): a double-stranded transition molecule (green) carries a
single-stranded overhang complementary to the exposed single-stranded end of the
remaining input molecule (blue). That exposed end (amber) encodes the current
state and symbol (q, a).
Stage 2 (ligate, then cut): the overhangs pair and ligase seals the two nicks,
giving one continuous duplex. An enzyme (gold) binds a recognition site in the
transition part and cuts both strands at a fixed offset downstream, in a
staggered pattern (red marks), inside the input region.
Stage 3 (expose the next end): a fragment is released as waste (grey), and the
remaining input, now one symbol shorter, carries a newly exposed end (amber)
encoding the next state and symbol. Lengths, sequences and enzyme-specific
offsets are intentionally not specified; this is not a laboratory protocol.
