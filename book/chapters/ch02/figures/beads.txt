Figure: the two bead operations in the historical workflow, in three panels.

Panel A (prepare a single strand): a grey bead is coated with streptavidin
(gold diamond). Biotin (black dot) sits on the 5' end of the strand made from
the complementary-O6 primer, so that strand is attached to the bead. Its
partner strand is paired with it. After denaturation, the attached strand stays
on the bead and the partner strand is released into solution; the released free
strand is the material carried into vertex selection.
Panel B (recognise, then wash): a bead carries, through biotin and
streptavidin, a probe strand complementary to a vertex code Oi. A target strand
containing Oi pairs with the probe and is retained. A strand lacking Oi does not
pair and is washed away (dashed arrow).
Panel C (release): denaturation separates the retained target from the probe;
the target returns to solution for the next vertex test while the bead keeps
its probe.
Legend: biotin-streptavidin binding provides attachment; complementary pairing
with the probe provides recognition.
