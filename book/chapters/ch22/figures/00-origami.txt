Figure: DNA origami, in three panels.

Panel A (one scaffold, many staples): a single long blue scaffold strand runs
from its 5' end back and forth through six parallel rows (helices), turning at
alternate ends, and finishes at its 3' end. Short coloured staple strands each
pair with two neighbouring rows: each runs along one row, crosses over, and
runs back along the next, pinning the rows together. Staples bridging different
row pairs are staggered so that the whole sheet is connected.
Panel B (a staple crossover): two adjacent scaffold helices run in opposite
directions. A rose staple, with its 5' end at upper right, pairs antiparallel to
the upper helix, crosses over to the lower helix, and pairs antiparallel there,
ending at its 3' end at lower right.
Panel C (staples as addressable pixels): a rectangle of staple positions about
100 nm across. Staples at chosen addresses carry an extra hairpin and appear as
raised dark pixels spelling DNA; the remaining positions are plain staples.
Helix twist, crossover spacing and sequence are omitted; the drawing is
schematic.
