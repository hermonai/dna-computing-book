Figure: clearing is many-to-one.
Panel A: three copies of register 000 and five copies of register 001 (a sticker
on address 2) are both cleared at bit 2, giving eight copies of 000. All eight
backbones remain, but which state each came from is lost.
Panel B: keeping a history bit h in another address before clearing preserves
the distinction: (b, h) = (0, 0) becomes (0, 0) and (b, h) = (1, 0) becomes
(0, 1), at the cost of one more address and more operations.
