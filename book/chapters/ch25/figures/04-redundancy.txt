Figure: one parity shard repairs one located erasure.
Panel A: three addressed shards each carry four bits: A = 1010, B = 0110 and the
parity shard P = A xor B = 1100; in every column A xor B xor P = 0.
Panel B: the address shows that shard A is missing (dashed); B and P are present,
so A = P xor B = 1010 is recovered.
Panel C: two cases parity cannot repair. If all three shards are present but P is
corrupted to 1101, the syndrome 0001 shows that something is wrong but not where,
so the group is rejected. If two shards are absent, one equation has two
unknowns and recovery is underdetermined.
