Figure: why toehold length controls strand-displacement speed, in two panels.

Panel A (schematic free-energy landscape): free energy is plotted against
reaction progress for three toehold lengths, 2, 4 and 6 nucleotides. Each curve
starts at the unbound invader plus gate, rises over a small initiation barrier,
drops into a well when the toehold binds, then crosses a long plateau of many
small, similar barriers (the reversible steps of branch migration), and finally
drops when the product strand is released. Longer toeholds give deeper wells.
A deeper well means the bound invader detaches less often, so more binding
events complete migration.
Panel B (toehold length sets the rate): a schematic curve of effective rate
constant (log scale) against toehold length from 0 to 10 nucleotides rises
roughly exponentially for short toeholds, spanning about a millionfold, and then
saturates near the hybridisation rate for long toeholds. The curve is a
qualitative summary, not fitted data.
