Figure: redundancy helps only when failures are independent.
Panel A: five independent copies of logical bit 1 read 1, 1, 0, 1, 0. A majority
vote returns 1: two flipped copies are outvoted, and for small per-copy error p
the failure probability is about 10 p^3 (five choose three).
Panel B: a common-mode fault flips all five copies at once, so the failure
probability is still p, not of order p^3; five copies are not five independent
trials. The vote is an external mathematical decoder; a molecular system must
realise redundancy through its own assembly or proofreading pathways, such as
proofreading tile sets.
