Figure: how DNA molecules become measurements, in two panels.

Panel A (gel electrophoresis): a gel slab sits in a buffer tank between a
negative electrode at the top and a positive electrode at the bottom. Samples
are loaded in wells at the top. Because DNA is negatively charged it migrates
towards the positive electrode, and shorter fragments travel further; migration
distance is roughly linear in the logarithm of length. Lane L is a size ladder
(1000, 500, 300, 200 and 100 base pairs). Lane 1 shows one band at about
300 bp; lane 2 two bands at about 500 and 200 bp; lane 3 a doublet within a
smear near 300 bp, indicating more than one species; lane 4 one band at about
100 bp.
Panel B (fluorescence reporter): before reaction, a fluorophore (star, F) on
one strand sits next to a quencher (black dot, Q) on its partner, so the
duplex is dark. When an output strand carrying F is released, F separates from
Q and the solution brightens. A plot of signal against time rises and
saturates. The signal is a bulk average over many molecules; calibration is
needed to turn it into a concentration.
