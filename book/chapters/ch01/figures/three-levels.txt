Figure: three levels of a DNA computation, drawn as three horizontal bands.

Formal level (top, teal): the instance X is the six-vertex directed teaching
graph with vertices A to F. The specification asks whether a route from A to F
visits every vertex exactly once; a proposed witness such as ABCDEF can be
checked in a number of steps proportional to the number of vertices.
Physical level (middle, blue): an "encode" arrow leads down to a tube holding a
population M with many copies of many routes. Reaction arrows lead through
joining by ligase, copying by PCR, separation on a gel and capture on a bead.
A red note warns that each step has finite yield and can keep wrong molecules.
Interpretive level (bottom, violet): a "measure" arrow leads to an observation
O, a staircase of gel bands. Observation arrows lead to a decoded order
ABCDEF and then to a claim: a witness exists, checked against X; a missing band
would not prove that none exists. An "interpret and verify" arrow returns to the
formal level, closing the loop.
