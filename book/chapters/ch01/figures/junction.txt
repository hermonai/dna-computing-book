Figure: how two edge strands are joined at vertex B.

Panel A (annealing). Two upper strands are written 5' to 3' from left to right.
The first represents the edge A to B and carries domains aR and bL; the second
represents the edge B to C and carries domains bR and cL. Beneath them, the
vertex splint for B runs antiparallel (3' at left, 5' at right) and carries the
complementary domains bL* and bR*. Grey rungs show pairing of bL with bL* and
of bR with bR*. Pairing places the 3' end of the first edge strand beside the 5'
end of the second; the gap in the backbone between them is a nick (red marker:
a 3' hydroxyl beside a 5' phosphate).
Panel B (ligation). After ligase acts, the upper strand is one continuous
molecule reading ... aR bL bR cL ...; a gold dot marks the new backbone bond.
The splint is still paired underneath and can later be removed by melting.
Domain lengths are schematic and chemical end groups other than the nick are
omitted.
