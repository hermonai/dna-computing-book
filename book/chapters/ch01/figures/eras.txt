Figure: four overlapping eras of DNA computing, drawn as four coloured cards
above a time axis from 1994 to 2026.

1994-2003, search in a tube (blue): a tube and a gel icon. Libraries of
candidate strands are filtered by PCR, gels and capture probes. Examples:
Hamiltonian path, satisfiability, maximal clique, sticker memory, a 20-variable
3-SAT instance. Lesson: parallelism does not remove exponential cost (Part I).
2001-2010, molecular machines (rose): an enzyme cutting a duplex and a small
tile pattern. Enzymes and tiles act autonomously: automata step through states,
tiles grow patterns, a scaffold folds into shapes. Examples: enzyme-driven
automata, Sierpinski crystals, DNA origami, enzyme-free logic. Lesson: local
rules compose (Parts IV-V).
2006-2019, programmed dynamics (green): an invading strand displacing an
incumbent and a sigmoidal output curve. Strand displacement lets sequence
program reaction rates: circuits, reaction networks, neural networks, walkers
and robots. Examples: seesaw circuits, Hopfield and winner-take-all networks, a
cargo-sorting robot, data storage. Lesson: interfaces, leak and fuel (Parts
V-VI).
2019-2026, learning and integration (violet): a small neural network and a
grid. Reprogrammable hardware, molecular learning, rechargeable circuits,
recording in cells. Examples: a 21-algorithm tile set, gate arrays, learning in
vitro, epigenetic-bit storage, a scaffolded computer. Lesson: see the Epilogue.
The eras overlap; dates indicate when each style of work became prominent, not
sharp boundaries.
