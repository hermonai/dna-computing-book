Figure: filter order matters when filters have capacity.
A pool contains a bad molecule followed by a good one, in fixed order. Filter F
has one slot and admits either identity; filter G admits only good molecules and
has two slots. Each filter keeps what it admits and passes it on.
Top row, F then G: the bad molecule takes F's single slot, G rejects it, and the
output is empty. Bottom row, G then F: G keeps only the good molecule, F admits
it, and the output is good. The rule is a deterministic counterexample, not a
kinetic model of bead competition.
