# Chapter 4 storyboard and claim plan

Author-approved production plan, 13 September 2026. Standalone LaTeX candidate;
does not alter accepted chapters or the concurrent Chapters 1-3 textbook migration.

1. Multiplicity versus diversity. Draw eight strand molecules but only four labeled
sequence identities, with one identity absent in a second sample. Molecule count
is not coverage. Color reinforced by sequence labels; no atomistic claim.
2. Recognition is not sealing. Antiparallel template plus two aligned fragments,
a 3-prime hydroxyl/5-prime phosphate nick, ligase and a continuous product backbone.
Solid backbone versus dashed base recognition. Conditional yield is not guaranteed
by the drawn geometric match. ATP-dependent example, not every ligase cofactor.
3. Miss probability. Curves from the declared independent-copy calculation, with
a shared-failure floor as a countermodel. Axes: copies and dimensionless probability;
no fitted kinetics or measured assay. Explain the independence that fails.
4. Concentration and volume. Equal molecule counts in different volumes, followed
by an ideal pseudo-first-order reaction-time comparison. Molecules are not processors.
5. Aliquot and capture. Population, fractional sample, retained target and readout
as distinct transformations; captured molecule identity survives the panels.
Sampling loss and detection are not a proof that no solution exists.
6. Resource audit. Work/depth/material/readout comparison as a dimensioned table,
not a scalar 'speedup'. Generated budget table compares factorial state space
against physical copies and volume; no universal DNA lower bound.

Core derivations: Bernoulli miss; minimal budget; expected distinct identities;
all-identity union bound; nonuniform rare species; without-replacement sampling;
conditional recovery; batch-failure floor; molarity and volume; ideal reaction-time
scaling; positive-predictive-value example. Closed worked exercises plus open assay
audit rubric. Original numerical models, BIPM unit definition, manufacturer ligation
mechanism; no claimed wet-lab result.
