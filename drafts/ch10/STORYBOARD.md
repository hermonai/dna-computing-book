# Chapter 10 storyboard: separation, readout, and measurement

Author-approved scope: infer what an observation can establish, starting with two
different 500 bp products. All figures are original editable TikZ; all numerical
plots come from the tested model. No image is experimental data.

1. Molecular sieve: negatively charged duplex fragments in a polymer mesh,
   migration toward the positive electrode, electric field in the opposite
   direction. Short/long fragments are geometry, not calibrated trajectories.
   Risk: never draw the gel as cutting DNA or identifying sequence.
2. Ladder and calibration: paired ladder lane and distance/log-length axes.
   Known lengths anchor interpolation; a sample band locates an interval.
   Risk: local relation is not a universal electrophoretic law.
3. Band formation: two Gaussian species profiles, their sum, and detector bins.
   Counts become length-weighted integrated signal. Preserve material in tails.
   Risk: numerical bands must be labeled synthetic, not laboratory photographs.
4. Identifiability: equal-length sequence-distinct duplexes, identical gel
   response columns, and a separately calibrated discriminatory channel.
   Risk: illustrative cross-reactivity is not a validated probe.
5. Collection window: target and contaminant profiles with a selected spatial
   interval. Show target recovery versus molecular purity on computed curves.
   Risk: ideal window capture omits handling and extraction losses.

Derive calibrated migration, bin integration, weighted inversion, correlated
background error, saturation, and the purity/recovery trade-off. Provide a
rank-deficient counterexample, uncertainty growth near ambiguity, and controls.
