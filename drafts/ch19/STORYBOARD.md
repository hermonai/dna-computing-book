# Chapter 19 storyboard — 27 September 2026

Author-approved scope before prose: mechanisms, first-passage theory, finite-pool
conversion and leakage, not a sequence-level laboratory protocol. All numeric
rates are assigned. Five editable plates have semantic TXT companions.

1. **01-exchange** — Which bonds change? Three aligned panels retain one
   antiparallel substrate while an invader binds a toehold, advances the branch
   point and releases an incumbent. Named domains, polarity, continuous backbone
   strokes and short base-pair rungs distinguish association from strand cutting.
   Risk: never imply polymerase synthesis, ligation or cleavage is required.
2. **02-walk** — Why does binding not ensure release? A chain of transient states
   j=0..m-1, absorbing detached D and released P states, named migration and
   detachment rates. Caption states detachment-only-at-zero simplification.
3. **03-commitment** — How does dissociation change success? Computed p0 versus
   assigned detachment rate at two branch lengths; exact boundary-value solution
   and its uniform-rate closed form are independently checked.
4. **04-inventory** — Where do strands go? Initial I+G and final W+O retain the
   same three physical strands, with separate invariant ledgers. Molecular species
   counts must not be confused with strand counts or arbitrary Boolean fan-out.
5. **05-leak** — When can a reporter separate inputs? Generated no-input and
   input-present conversion curves share finite gate inventory. A threshold line
   and crossing interpretation expose finite readout windows, not a measured assay.

Risk checks: polarity, no invented bases, stochastic boundaries, reversible versus
absorbing approximations, absolute versus conditional probability, concentration
units, and experimental controls. General network solvers remain Chapter 28.
